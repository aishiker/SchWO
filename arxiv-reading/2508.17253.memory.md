## Project relevance

This paper is directly relevant to Q018.  It studies scalar scattering at
finite observer radius and argues that the partial-wave series naturally
truncates around `ell_max ~ k r` when the finite-radius radial function is
used instead of an asymptotic radial expansion.

For this project the result should be treated as:

- strong evidence for a finite-radius high-ell cutoff/tail policy;
- not by itself a proof for the full spin-2 RW/Zerilli gravitational-wave
  observable, because metric reconstruction, parity sectors, tensor
  harmonics, and polarization extraction can change prefactors and validation
  needs.

## Key formulas / facts

- Frequency convention in the paper uses `exp(-i k t)` in the time-domain
  Fourier factor, consistent with the project convention.
- Incident scalar plane wave is expanded as
  `exp(i k r cos(theta)) = sum_ell i^ell (2ell+1) j_ell(k r) P_ell(cos theta)`.
- The exact finite-radius spherical-Bessel expansion is convergent at any
  finite `r`; empirically its natural cutoff is `ell_max ~ k r`.
- The usual large-`kr` asymptotic approximation to `j_ell(kr)` is valid for
  `kr >> ell`, not merely `kr >> 1`.  Applying it to `ell ~ kr` modes creates
  the partial-wave divergence.
- For finite-radius black-hole scalar scattering, the spin-0 RW potential is
  `V_ell = f(r) [ell(ell+1)/r^2 + 2M/r^3]`.
- The finite-radius Schwarzschild scalar wave is summed with the numerical
  radial function, not with an asymptotic radial function at the observer.
- The paper states that for an observer at finite `r`, PWS is truncated
  empirically at `ell_max ~ k r`, and radial equations are solved for
  `ell <= ell_max`.
- Physical interpretation: low-ell modes have turning points well inside the
  observer radius and reach the observer as propagating waves; high-ell modes
  with turning points beyond the observer are suppressed by the centrifugal
  barrier and do not significantly reach the detector.
- Sec. V notes that for finite source and observer radii the expected scaling
  is `ell_max ~ k min(r, r_s)`.
- The paper explicitly leaves full gravitational-wave scattering, including
  gauge, polarization distortion, and helicity issues, to future work.
