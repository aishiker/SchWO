## Paper roadmap

- **Abstract / overview.** Finite-radius Schwarzschild black-hole perturbation
  theory is used to avoid the forward partial-wave divergence produced by
  radial asymptotic expansions. The paper reconstructs GW polarizations and
  compares them with asymptotic scattering and point-lens Kirchhoff results.
- **I. Introduction.** Motivates strong-field GW lensing beyond geometric
  optics and scalar Kirchhoff diffraction. States that Kirchhoff methods omit
  polarization evolution and long-range/strong-field effects.
- **II. Black-hole perturbation theory.** Introduces RW-gauge tensor-harmonic
  decomposition, odd/even Regge-Wheeler and Zerilli master variables,
  Schwarzschild potentials, tortoise coordinate, and horizon/infinity
  boundary conditions.
- **III. Plane gravitational waves.** Expands a +z TT plane wave with complex
  amplitudes `A_plus`, `A_cross` in tensor harmonics; derives odd/even incident
  coefficients and the flat-space limit used to normalize scattering modes.
- **IV. Scattered gravitational waves.** Reconstructs metric components from
  the master variables, constructs strict NP Weyl scalars in a Kinnersley
  tetrad, rotates to an incident-aligned Cartesian tetrad, and defines
  geodesic-deviation polarizations.
- **IV.1 Metric reconstruction.** Gives reconstruction operators acting on
  odd/even master functions and their radial derivatives.
- **IV.2 Weyl scalars.** Defines the strict NP quintuple, the two-sided Fourier
  representation, mode sums, radial `Z_n` functions, and the tetrad rotation.
- **IV.3 GW polarization.** Eq. (42) defines real-time +, cross, vector,
  breathing, and longitudinal accelerations from real/imaginary parts of the
  incident-frame NP scalars. The incident direction is used as the tetrad
  direction even though a scattered wave has no unique local propagation
  direction.
- **V. Numerical results.** Uses `A_plus=0.9+1.1i`,
  `A_cross=0.4+0.6i`, frequencies `0.1/M` through `4/M`, and finite-radius
  partial-wave sums.
- **V.1 Convergence.** Shows why radial asymptotics fail when `kr` is not much
  larger than ell, and demonstrates convergence of the finite-radius sums.
- **V.2 Wave field.** Presents Fig. 3 and discusses the Poisson spot and
  distorted wavefronts.
- **V.3 Diffraction pattern.** Presents Fig. 4 and compares finite-radius
  exact fields with regularized asymptotic scattering.
- **V.4 Transmission factor.** Defines `F_plus,F_cross` via the ratio of
  lensed to incident complex frequency-domain waveforms, introduces the
  scalar point-lens Kirchhoff expression, and presents Figs. 5-6 at the eight
  Table-I points.
- **VI. Summary.** Reiterates the finite-radius method, the failure of
  asymptotic/Kirchhoff approximations in relevant regimes, and the role of
  polarization evolution.
- **Appendix A.** Tensor and spin-weighted spherical-harmonic conventions.
- **Appendix B.** Gauge transformations and RW gauge fixing.
- **Appendix C.** Apparent vector/scalar polarization wave fields (Fig. 7),
  explicitly described as environmental/tetrad effects rather than new
  propagating degrees of freedom.
- **Appendix D.** Gives explicit complex plus/cross partial-wave amplitudes
  (Eq. 55), distorted-plane plus reflected-wave decomposition, and the
  left/right helicity conversion matrix.
- **Appendix E.** Series-reduction regularization and the differential cross
  section used in Fig. 8.

## Key equations and convention traps

- **Eq. (2), (32):** two-sided Fourier convention `exp(-ikt)`.
- **Eq. (11)-(12):** radial ingoing/outgoing boundary conditions.
- **Eq. (21)-(22), (27):** odd/even incident coefficients from complex +/cross
  amplitudes and left/right helicities.
- **Eq. (30):** strict NP Weyl tensor contractions.
- **Eq. (34)-(35):** strict NP mode sums and radial `Z_n` functions. The stars
  in Eq. (35g,h) require a full `(-k,-m)` reality interpretation; v1 does not
  spell out a software-ready positive-frequency mapping.
- **Eq. (39)-(40):** incident-tetrad rotation through Wigner-D coefficients;
  an implementation must independently freeze active/passive and phase
  conventions.
- **Eq. (42):** real-time geodesic-deviation accelerations,
  `ddot h_plus = Re Psi4_hat + Re Psi0_hat` and
  `ddot h_cross = -(Im Psi4_hat - Im Psi0_hat)`.
- **Eq. (45)-(46):** `h_tilde_p = F_p A_tilde_p exp(ik r cos(theta))`.
- **Eq. (47):** printed Kirchhoff factor uses `exp(+pi gamma/2)` with
  `gamma=-2Mk`. This sign makes the magnitude exponentially decay and is
  inconsistent with the plotted Figs. 5-6. The standard/figure-consistent
  expression for this gamma convention uses `exp(-pi gamma/2)`; the ratio is
  the positive real factor `exp(2 pi Mk)`, so the phase is unchanged.
- **Eq. (55a,b):** explicit complex far-zone plus/cross partial-wave
  amplitudes in terms of even/odd master functions. These strongly support
  the positive-frequency continuation
  `h_plus=-(Psi4_hat+Psi0_hat)/k^2` and
  `h_cross=-i(Psi4_hat-Psi0_hat)/k^2`, while leaving the finite-radius
  `(-k,-m)` implementation details under-specified.
- **Eq. (57)-(60):** reflected-wave helicity conversion and parity-dependent
  mixing, useful as a far-zone consistency check rather than a unique local
  finite-radius prescription.

## Fig. 5/6 inference relevant to this project

1. The paper gives an implicit exact definition through Eqs. (32), (42),
   (45), and an explicit far-zone complex formula in Eq. (55); it does not
   provide one software-ready finite-radius line resolving all negative-
   frequency, `m -> -m`, conjugation, and Wigner-D phases.
2. The most strongly supported complex amplitudes are
   `h_plus=-(Psi4_hat+Psi0_hat)/k^2` and
   `h_cross=-i(Psi4_hat-Psi0_hat)/k^2`, using strict incident-frame NP
   scalars with the complete reality bridge.
3. The repository Route-B projection gives, in strict-NP variables,
   `h_plus_RB=0.5*h_plus_paper-Psi2/k^2` and
   `h_cross_RB=0.5*h_cross_paper`. The factor 0.5 cancels in a cross
   transmission ratio, but the complex Psi2 contamination does not cancel in
   the plus ratio. This predicts a much larger current F_plus discrepancy.
4. The Kirchhoff amplitude mismatch in both figures is independently closed
   by the Eq. (47) exponential-sign inconsistency. Corrected ranges at the
   four far-axis points are approximately `[0.662,1.527]`, `[0.770,1.290]`,
   `[0.843,1.189]`, and `[0.891,1.123]`, reproducing the plotted oscillation
   scales. Its phase is identical to the printed-sign version.
5. Remaining far-axis F_cross disagreement points to the unresolved strict
   lower-NP/reality and tetrad-phase bridge, not to the Kirchhoff sign or a
   simple renderer error.
